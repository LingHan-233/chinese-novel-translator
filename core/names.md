### Names and special terms

**The tables in this task always win.** Use their English exactly. Only choose a new English form for a name that is not in the tables, and then list it as a TERM/CHAR line in your notes so it stays the same in later passages.

Three kinds of names:

1. **People's names → pinyin**, surname first, given name written as one word, no tone marks: 林凡 → Lin Fan, 欧阳明月 → Ouyang Mingyue. Nicknames: 老王 → Old Wang; 小凡 → Xiao Fan (or as the character table says).
2. **Names with a meaning** (sects, places, techniques, treasures, titles, epithets) → short English that keeps the image, capitalised: 青云宗 → Azure Cloud Sect, 天剑峰 → Heavenly Sword Peak, 血手人屠 → the Bloody-Handed Butcher.
3. **Concepts readers of the genre already know** (qi, dantian, jianghu, Dao, yin and yang) → keep the pinyin, lowercase, no italics.

**Title prefixes are not names:** 代 = acting, 副 = deputy/vice, 前 = former, 大/二/三 + kinship or rank = eldest/second/third. 秦代掌门 → Acting Sect Leader Qin (never "Qin Dai"); 张副县令 → Deputy Magistrate Zhang.

First time a reader meets something they cannot guess, add a few words inside the sentence, once: "his dantian, the reservoir of energy below his navel". After that, use the bare term.

Real brands, apps and places keep their usual English names (微信 → WeChat, 北京 → Beijing).
