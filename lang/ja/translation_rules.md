DRAFT (v0.9.0): written by the developer, not yet checked by a Japanese reader. Keep it short. -->
In order of importance:

1. **Everything, nothing more.** Translate every sentence into Japanese. Do not add, explain, soften or summarise.
2. **Keep the voice.** A rough character stays rough, a formal one stays formal, a joking narrator keeps joking. Match the strength of insults and swearing. Choose each character's first-person word (俺, 僕, 私, わし...) and speech level once, to fit them, and keep it.
3. **Natural Japanese, same rhythm.** Write it the way a Japanese novel reads: narration in plain style (だ・である, mostly past tense), dialogue in natural spoken Japanese. Rebuild sentences freely, but keep the paragraph breaks. Short one-line paragraphs are deliberate pacing: keep them short.
4. **Subjects.** Japanese leaves out obvious subjects, so do not write 彼は/彼女は in every sentence. When it is not clear who acts, use the name or the role (少年, 執事). Use the character table for 彼/彼女.
5. **Idioms (成语, 俗语): translate what they do.** Use a Japanese 四字熟語 only if it really means the same thing in Japanese. Watch for words that look the same but mean something else in Japanese (东西, 手纸, 大丈夫, 汽车, 爱人, 工作, 勉强, 新闻, 走): translate the Chinese meaning.
6. **Sound words** (哼, 嘿嘿, 轰): use Japanese ones (ふん, へへっ, ドォン) or describe the action (鼻を鳴らした). Never leave the Chinese sound characters (哼, 啊, 嘿).
7. **Stacked phrases and repetition** used only for emphasis: compress into one strong phrase. Repetition that matters to the story (a catchphrase, a callback) stays.
8. **Dialogue:** 「...」; a quote inside dialogue 『...』. Chinese quotation marks (“...”, "...", 「...」) all become 「...」; book titles 《...》 become 『...』. Make clear who speaks.
9. **No notes in the text.** No brackets, footnotes, readings in parentheses or 訳注. If a reader needs a hint, weave a few words into the sentence the first time.
10. **Don't improve the author.** Keep deliberate vagueness, clichés of the genre, cliffhangers and secrets. Never reveal something the original hides.
11. **Wordplay and homophones (谐音, 双关):** rebuild the joke in Japanese (a Japanese pun or word with the same double meaning). If nothing works, keep the meaning and add a FLAG line in your notes.
12. **Stand-in words (谐音/委婉替代):** web novels often use an innocent word for a rude or taboo one (牛至 "oregano" for 牛子, 河蟹 for 和谐, cute doubled words like 牛牛). Never translate the literal word (オレガノ): use a Japanese euphemism of the same register (ムスコ) and keep it the same every time. Check the slang table first; its suggestions may be in English: use the Japanese equivalent. If a literal reading makes no sense in the scene, suspect a stand-in. If the book keeps using one that is not in the table, add a SLANG line to your notes.
13. **Special lines:** system/game panels 【...】 stay 【...】; image placeholders (﹝图﹞, [图片], 插图) → ［挿絵］, followed by any text they hold; scene breaks (***, ……, ◆◆◆) → ＊　＊　＊; keep letters, lists and chat messages as separate lines like the original.
14. **Japanese characters only.** Write every kanji in its Japanese form (剑→剣, 灵→霊, 门→門, 师→師, 气→気, 龙→龍, 苏→蘇, 刘→劉). A Chinese word Japanese does not write with kanji becomes ordinary Japanese (躲 → 隠れる). Punctuation: 。、！？, ellipsis ……, dash ――.
