### Names and special terms

**The tables in this task always win.** Use their Japanese exactly. Only choose a new form for a name that is not in the tables, and then list it as a TERM/CHAR line in your notes so it stays the same in later passages.

Japanese readers can read Chinese characters, so most names keep their kanji:

1. **People's names → the same characters in Japanese form.** No katakana reading, no reading in brackets: 林凡 → 林凡, 苏晴 → 蘇晴, 欧阳明月 → 欧陽明月. Nicknames keep their form (小凡 → 小凡, 老王 → 老王) unless the character table says otherwise.
2. **Names with a meaning** (sects, places, techniques, treasures, titles, epithets) → the same kanji in Japanese form: 青云宗 → 青雲宗, 天剑峰 → 天剣峰, 血手人屠 → 血手人屠. Rewrite in Japanese words only if the kanji would mislead a Japanese reader.
3. **Concepts readers of the genre already know** (气, 丹田, 江湖, 道, 阴阳) → the kanji in Japanese form: 気, 丹田, 江湖, 道, 陰陽.

**Title prefixes are not names:** 代 = 代理, 副 = 副, 前 = 前; 秦代掌门 → 秦掌門代理 or 代理掌門の秦 (never a person called 秦代); 张副县令 → 張副県令.

First time a reader meets something they cannot guess, add a few words inside the sentence, once ("臍の下にある気の集まる場所、丹田"). After that, use the bare term.

Real brands, apps and places use their usual Japanese names (微信 → WeChat, 支付宝 → アリペイ, 北京 → 北京).
