Aim: fluent modern English fiction, the way a well-edited web-novel translation reads. American spelling. Past tense and the same point of view as the original. Contractions in dialogue.

Avoid these translationese habits — use the natural alternative:

| Chinese habit | Don't write | Write |
|---|---|---|
| 一丝 | "a trace of a smile" every time | "a faint smile", or just "he smiled" |
| 不禁 / 忍不住 | "couldn't help but" every time | vary: "found himself", or drop it |
| 顿时 / 瞬间 | "instantly", "in an instant" | often nothing; let the action be sudden |
| 淡淡道 / 冷冷道 | "said faintly", "said coldly" | let the line carry the tone; at most "he said flatly" |
| 冷哼一声 | "snorted coldly" | "He snorted." |
| 脸色一变 | "his face changed" | "his expression darkened / he paled" (choose by context) |
| 心中一惊 | "his heart was shocked" | "he was startled" |
| 嘴角微微上扬 | "the corner of his mouth slightly rose" | "a smile tugged at his lips" |
| 说道 / 道 | "said" after every line | use "said" sparingly; action beats or nothing |

Keep the energy: exclamation marks only where the original shouts; no ellipsis chains beyond what the original has.
